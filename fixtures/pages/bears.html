How bears get ready for winter

In late summer and autumn, bears enter a phase called hyperphagia: they eat and drink almost without stopping. A black bear can take in up to 20,000 calories a day, mostly berries, nuts and acorns, and may forage for 20 hours a day. The fat it puts on is the only fuel it will have for months.

When food runs out, usually between October and December depending on the region, bears settle in a den: a hollow tree, a cave, a hole dug in a hillside or under the roots of a fallen tree. Snow is not required, but it insulates the den.

During hibernation a bear does not eat, drink, urinate or defecate. Its heart rate falls from 40 to 50 beats a minute to as low as 8, and it breathes about once every 45 seconds. Its body temperature only drops by a few degrees, which lets it wake up quickly if it is disturbed. A bear can lose 15 to 30 percent of its body weight over the winter.

Cubs are born in the den in January or February. They weigh less than half a kilogram at birth, blind and almost hairless, and nurse while their mother keeps hibernating. The family leaves the den in spring, between March and May.

Not every bear hibernates: in places where food is available all winter, some stay active. Polar bears are the exception among bears, since only pregnant females den for the winter.
